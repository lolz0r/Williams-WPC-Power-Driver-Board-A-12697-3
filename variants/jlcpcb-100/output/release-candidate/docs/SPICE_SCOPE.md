# Simulation scope and reproducibility

These are ngspice engineering models, not a manufacturer-validated whole-board simulation. The supply controller uses an averaged model because the public TI PSpice switching model is encrypted. Transistor/diode fits, load envelopes, thermal boundaries and transformer impedances are stated in each fixture. A completed transient must reach its requested stop time without simulator errors before measurements can pass. Earlier rejected selections and convergence trials remain under `output/verification`; only the suites explicitly listed in the final manifest are accepted results.

Decks and ngspice logs accompany each accepted report. Replays retain a SHA-256 of the source trace. The release includes a snapshot of the trace and relevant models, fixtures and scripts. Paths in archived decks identify the workspace used for the actual run; regenerate with the included scripts for a different checkout path. Do not rerun the inherited legacy `run_all.py` and interpret its old TPS54360 regulator fixtures as verification of the new controller.

Supply results do not validate PFM burst ripple, switching spikes, EMI, internal protection timing, reverse-power behavior, startup under every load, or actual silicon thermal behavior. Validate loaded rails, CPU reset margin, loop behavior and switching waveforms on the first article. A 100-board inventory target is not evidence that 100 boards should be built before that qualification.

The 100 V reservoir changes from 4,400 to 4,000 µF nominal. The final ripple model uses the actual four individually annotated NHA capacitors. Samyoung rates these 2.02 A at 120 Hz/105°C; the 50 Hz mains test applies a further conservative 0.9 factor. Low-voltage GPD capacitors are rated 5.6 A at 100 kHz/125°C, with manufacturer 0.85 factor at 120 Hz, plus a conservative 0.9 factor for the 100 Hz ripple test. Current sharing includes opposite capacitance tolerances and 2:1 ESR. These are sensitivities, not an exhaustive manufacturing distribution.

F112 cold inrush includes the board's 4,000 µF and an **assumed** external Fliptronic 4,400 µF, both at +20%. Winding resistance is swept over 0.6–1.2 Ω. The fuse's 139.419 A²s value is typical pre-arcing data; comparing a modeled cold start to that number does not establish repetitive pulse endurance, guaranteed opening time or fault protection. Actual transformer prospective fault current, external loads, fuse derating and coordination remain physical acceptance gates.

The MOT dual rectifier has a 175°C junction rating. Its hot forward-voltage bound uses the manufacturer's 100/150°C typical curves with added voltage margin and 10% current margin, rather than the earlier single-leg cold fit. Its junction-to-case value is not specified in the archived sheet; the thermal sensitivity uses an explicit 4°C/W engineering assumption. These four rectifiers are screened at 150°C (25°C below rating), while the175°C-rated onsemi MBRB20H100CTT4G SMT rectifiers remain screened at125°C, where their maximum leakage is specified. Thus the old common 125°C target is not preserved for the MOT devices. Physical case-temperature measurements and supplier thermal data are required to validate this choice. The onsemi hot loss line is0.55V+0.009Ω×I per leg, through the125°C maximum forward-voltage points0.64V/10A and0.73V/20A; low-current interpolation is an engineering bound checked against the maximum curve. The thermal bound uses3mA per leg (6mA per package) at the full rail peak for the entire cycle. This bounds the manufacturer’s **maximum** reverse-current curve at125°C for the actual rails below30V; that curve is below2mA/leg through30V. It is not the typical curve. The6mA/leg tabulated limit applies at100V. Junction-to-pad thermal resistance is2°C/W. The final bridge and capacitor ripple suites use a deck-local lower-drop rectifier model to capture increased charging current. Supply-headroom fixtures retain the earlier higher-drop model as a conservative source-voltage case. Neither fit replaces a silicon or transformer measurement. All dual-leg maximum leakage is included conservatively. Heatsink orientation, interfaces and neighboring heat must match the intended assembly.

The copper thermal model is a finite-volume estimate with unmeasured convection/radiation coefficients. It does not establish lifetime, safety certification or cabinet thermal qualification. Complete the first-article acceptance procedure before authorizing the remaining production quantity.

The earlier Baocheng thermal result is retained as `thermal_baocheng_rejected.json` in the working verification directory and is excluded from the accepted package. The final part was selected because both loss and leakage matter; its stock snapshot provides1,746 available versus1,260 required including reserve. Manufacturer data: [onsemi MBR20H100CT family, Rev13](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/mbr20h100ct-d.pdf); the exact tape/reel MBRB20H100CTT4G remains in the active ordering table.

The stiffest+20% capacitance crest-start fixture produces244.752A in the more heavily loaded rectifier leg, close to its250A single-pulse rating. The fixture explicitly includes opposite20% leg resistance and10:1 saturation-current mismatch. This small surge margin makes real transformer impedance, cold/hot starts and current-sharing measurements mandatory before a100-board production commitment. The less conservative0.1Ω loop corner has substantially lower surge.
