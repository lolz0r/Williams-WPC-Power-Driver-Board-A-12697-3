# First-article acceptance before the 100-board build

The inventory audit covers 100 boards plus the documented spares. It does not replace first-article qualification. Complete a small engineering build before committing the remaining quantity.

1. Have the fabricator review the four-layer stack, finished outer/inner copper, drill plating, native polarity and SMT orientation previews. Agree on the entire through-hole/manual BOM, prepared connector work and the soldered F112 fuse. Obtain the externally sourced mechanical hardware. Do not silently omit holders, cartridges, screws, nuts or heatsinks.
2. Inspect the received parts against exact manufacturer numbers and footprints. Verify the prepared TE headers against the actual keyed mating housings. Confirm J115's two-piece seam and J113 latch/cable clearance. Use a forming fixture and a meter to verify BR3's +, − and AC terminals against the new pad numbering before soldering. Inspect all capacitor polarities and ratings.
3. Inspect shorts, clearances, fuse values, solder joints and exposed-pad wetting. Verify heatsink interfaces, screw engagement/torque and live-tab clearance. Check cabinet and underside clearance with the actual assembled board and harnesses.
4. Bring up with protected, current-limited sources and the intended transformer connections. Measure rail startup, shutdown, prebias/restart, the loaded 5.1 V/3 A and 12 V/0.75 A rails, CPU-end voltage/reset margin and supply switching/loop waveforms. Check the high rail at measured worst mains regulation. Do not assume the behavioral model predicts switching overshoot or protection timing.
5. Exercise the real lamp, motor, flasher, coil and GI loads, including channels absent from the recorded trace. Confirm blanking inhibits outputs and that each GI triac commutates and triggers in the required quadrants. Measure drain overshoot and gate voltages at the actual harness loads.
6. Measure capacitor ripple and temperatures, semiconductor case/estimated junction temperatures, copper/connector temperatures and all ten heatsink temperatures after thermal soak in the cabinet. Correlate against the engineering thermal model and measured local ambient. Confirm MOT thermal resistance with the supplier or measured case data; do not treat the assumed 4°C/W junction-to-case value as a specification.
7. Qualify cold and hot starts, repeated start cycles, real Fliptronic branch loading and representative protected fault cases with the actual transformer. Check F112's temperature derating, interruption capability and coordination, and the REOMAX cartridge time-current behavior. Typical I²t comparisons alone are not acceptance evidence. Preserve the specified fuse ratings.

Record serial numbers, part lots, actual line/transformer impedances, harness configuration, instruments, waveforms and test results. Resolve failures by revising the design and regenerating the source-bound manufacturing package.
